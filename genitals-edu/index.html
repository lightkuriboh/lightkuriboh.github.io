TissueWeek 7FemaleMale
1. SRY on the Y chromosome (Yp11.2) drives testis determination and Sertoli / Leydig cell differentiation.
2. Testosterone. Fetal Leydig cells secrete testosterone from about Week 8.
3. 5α-reductase type 2 converts testosterone to dihydrotestosterone (DHT) within the target tissues of the external genitalia.
4. Androgen receptor signalling. DHT drives tubercle elongation, medial fusion of the urethral folds (enclosing the spongy urethra) and inferomedial fusion of the labioscrotal swellings.
5. Absence of DHT yields the default 46,XX pattern: a short tubercle, unfused folds (labia minora) and unfused swellings (labia majora) bordering an open vestibule.
Hypospadias
Incomplete fusion of the urethral folds leaves the external urethral meatus on the ventral aspect of the penis.
Androgen insensitivity syndrome (AIS)
46,XY with a nonfunctional androgen receptor; target tissues do not respond to testosterone or DHT, giving a female-typical external phenotype (complete AIS).
5α-reductase type 2 deficiency
Reduced conversion of testosterone to DHT causes under-virilization of the external genitalia in 46,XY individuals.
Congenital adrenal hyperplasia (46,XX)
Fetal adrenal androgen excess virilizes the external genitalia (clitoromegaly, partial labioscrotal fusion).
