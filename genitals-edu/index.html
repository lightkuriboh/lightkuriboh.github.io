Embryological Organogenesis

External genitalia homology reference guide based on Langman's Medical Embryology and Netter's Clinical Anatomy.

During the bipotential stage (Weeks 4–7), morphologically indistinguishable precursor tissues arise around the cloacal membrane. Subsequent sexual dimorphism is mediated entirely by hormonal signaling.

Divergence from the default female phenotype requires active, timed androgenic stimulation starting in Week 8:

1. SRY Gene Activation (Yp11.2)
Expression of SRY (Sex-determining Region Y) drives primitive sex cords to differentiate into Sertoli and Leydig cells in the embryonic testis.
2. Testosterone Synthesis
Leydig cells produce testosterone by gestational Week 8. Testosterone stabilises the mesonephric (Wolffian) internal ducts.
3. 5α-Reductase Type 2 & DHT Conversion
In external genital target mesenchyme, enzyme 5α-reductase type 2 reduces testosterone to Dihydrotestosterone (DHT), which has ~5× higher affinity for the Androgen Receptor (AR).
4. Ventral Midline Fusion
DHT stimulates rapid phallic elongation, medial migration and fusion of the urethral folds to enclose the penile urethra, and inferomedial migration and scrotal fusion of the labioscrotal swellings.
5. Absence of Androgens (Female Pathway)
Without DHT stimulation, the genital tubercle ceases rapid elongation (forming the clitoris), urethral folds remain unfused as labia minora, and labioscrotal swellings form labia majora bordering the open vestibule.
Hypospadias (1 in 250 male live births)

Incomplete ventral fusion of the urogenital (urethral) folds results in an abnormally positioned external urethral meatus along the ventral aspect of the penis (glandular, penile shaft, or perineal).

Androgen Insensitivity Syndrome (CAIS / PAIS)

46,XY karyotype with loss-of-function mutations in the AR gene. Despite high circulating testosterone and DHT, external tissues fail to respond, developing an anatomically female external phenotype with blind-ending vagina.

5α-Reductase Type 2 Deficiency (46,XY DSD)

Impaired conversion of testosterone to DHT causes under-virilization at birth (pseudovaginal perineoscrotal hypospadias). At puberty, surging testosterone levels can trigger virilization.

Congenital Adrenal Hyperplasia (CAH, 46,XX)

Deficiency of 21-hydroxylase results in cortisol deficit and compensatory adrenal androgen hypersecretion, causing virilization of female genitalia (clitoromegaly, partial labioscrotal fusion).
